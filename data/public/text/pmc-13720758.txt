A 48‐year‐old healthy male presented to the accident and emergency (AE) department with a 10‐day history of right flank pain radiating to the groin. The patient was slightly hypotensive but otherwise vitally stable. There was mild epigastric and right hypochondriac tenderness without any peritoneal signs. Lab workup revealed a white blood count (WBC) of 20 × 109/L (ref: 3.4 – 9.6 × 109/L). Multiple views of abdominal radiographs were done, showcasing a radiopaque needlelike structure in the right side of the abdomen and paravertebral planes, suspicious for a foreign body (Figure 1). The patient denied any recent procedures or ingestion of bones or other objects.
