Hemodynamically stable on admission with an unremarkable cardiac exam, the patient presented with lateral ST‐segment changes and elevated troponin‐T. Serial ECGs demonstrated persistent subtle lateral ST‐segment depression (< 0.5 mm in V5–V6; Figure 1). Initial labs were notable for HbA1c 5.7% and LDL‐C 142 mg/dL. Despite treatment with standard cardiovascular medications at the referring hospital, refractory angina accompanied by a rise in troponin T (8160 ng/L) led to the patient being classified as a very high‐risk NSTEMI profile. Furthermore, he was identified as a candidate for an immediate invasive strategy with CAG within 2 h (Table 1). The angiogram showed acute thrombotic occlusion at the ostium of the left PDA in the setting of a left‐dominant coronary circulation, resulting in extensive IPMI (Figure 2A,B). The hyperdominant‐LCx, which was widely patent and free of significant stenosis, supplied myocardial territories that are typically perfused by the right coronary artery (RCA) (Figure 2C). Additionally, the left anterior descending artery (LAD) showed two moderate atherosclerotic plaques (~50% stenosis) at the proximal and middle segments, which could potentially contribute to the subtle, non‐dynamic, STD observed in the lateral precordial leads (Figure 2C). The RCA revealed multiple proximal concentric stenoses; however, as it was not the infarct‐related artery, no emergent percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) was performed (Figure 2D).
