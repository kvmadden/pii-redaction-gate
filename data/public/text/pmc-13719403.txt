At a follow-up visit on September 30, 2020, the patient reported progressive enlargement of the visual obscuration in her left eye since the day following her initial presentation. BCVA remained 0.8 in the right eye but had deteriorated to hand motions at 60 cm in the left eye. Blood pressure (BP) was markedly elevated at 187/104 mmHg, although she denied symptoms suggestive of hypertensive emergency, including headache, dizziness, nausea, or chest pain. Slit-lamp biomicroscopic examination with a fundus lens disclosed mild vitreous hemorrhage and expansion of the subretinal hemorrhage, while repeat OCT demonstrated progressive worsening of the neurosensory retinal elevation with accumulating subretinal blood components in the left eye (Figure 2). Given the patient's refusal of intravitreal anti-VEGF therapy, systemic antihypertensive treatment was initiated with oral nifedipine controlled-release tablets (30 mg twice daily) and metoprolol succinate sustained-release tablets (47.5 mg once daily), and she was instructed to maintain strict bed rest to minimize the risk of further hemorrhage.
