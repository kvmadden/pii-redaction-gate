A 74-year-old gentleman presented to the emergency department with an acute onset of weakness involving both upper and lower limbs, accompanied by exertional chest pain and discomfort. He had a history of a fall at home due to dizziness. ECG showed anterolateral ischemia, and the troponin T level was 313. Echocardiography showed global hypokinesia of the left ventricle and regional wall motion abnormalities, with an ejection fraction of 45%. Coronary angiography demonstrated severe triple-vessel coronary artery disease (Figures 1, 2), and a diagnosis of ACS with NSTEMI was established. The patient was managed medically with heparin 5000 units (injection), a diuretic, and aspirin (tablet). Revascularization was not beneficial as the coronary arteries were thin and completely occluded.
