A 47‐year‐old female with a history of recent dental extractions of sixteen decayed teeth presented with one week of left facial numbness and swelling. She noted that the onset of her left facial numbness preceded her dental extractions by five days. Vital signs included a temperature of 98.2°F, blood pressure of 157/78 mmHg, heart rate of 78 bpm, and respiratory rate of 18 breaths/minute. On examination, she was in no acute distress and appeared nontoxic. Oral examination revealed left mandibular swelling, abnormal dentition with dental tenderness, and gingival swelling. Neurological examination was notable for reduced sensation to light touch in the left V1‐V2 region. Computed tomography (CT) of the head without contrast demonstrated hypodensities in the right parietal and occipital lobes. Brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) with and without contrast demonstrated an 11 × 18 mm enhancing lesion in the right parieto‐occipital region with mild diffusion restriction and vasogenic edema, as well as small enhancing lesions in the right frontal and parietal lobes (Figures 1A–D). There were no paramagnetic rim lesions or central vein signs. Given the patient’s history of extensive dental caries, there was a high concern for odontogenic brain abscesses due to spread from her dental infections, for which she received empiric antibiotic treatment with IV vancomycin, ceftriaxone, and metronidazole. Infectious workup was unrevealing, with negative blood cultures, serum QuantiFERON‐TB Gold test, nonreactive rapid plasma reagin (RPR), HIV screen, and cryptococcal antigen. CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis showed no signs of occult malignancy, and the patient was up to date with her cancer screenings.
