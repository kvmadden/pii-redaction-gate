On March 2, 2026, a 92 y.o. woman, Olena D. Luchanko, was
admitted to North Field Hospital with three days of dyspnea,
orthopnea, and swelling of both ankles. Her daughter, Claire
Ellison, shares the house and gave the admission history.
She described a gain of about five pounds, a new need to
sleep on two pillows, and a shorter walking distance inside
the house. There was no chest pain, no syncope, no fever,
and no change in stool or urine color. Home medicines were
furosemide 20 mg by mouth daily, carvedilol 6.25 mg twice
daily, losartan 25 mg daily, and levothyroxine 88 mcg each
morning. She has no known drug allergy.

Past history includes heart failure with preserved ejection
fraction, hypertension, stage 3 chronic kidney disease, and
hypothyroidism. She also has Raynaud phenomenon, present
for many years, with cold-triggered color change in the
fingers and no digital ulcer. Gloves have been enough, and
she takes no vasodilator for it. There has been no stroke,
no myocardial infarction, and no prior intubation.

On arrival the blood pressure was 168/90 mmHg, the pulse was
108 beats per minute, the respiratory rate was 24, and the
oxygen saturation was 90 percent on room air. She was
speaking in short phrases, with crackles halfway up both
lung fields, an elevated jugular venous pressure, and pitting
edema to the mid shins. The abdomen was soft. She was
afebrile and oriented to person and place.

Laboratory results included sodium 134 mmol/L, potassium
3.2 mmol/L, creatinine 1.6 mg/dL (baseline near 1.3 mg/dL),
blood urea nitrogen 32 mg/dL, BNP 1860 pg/mL, hemoglobin
11.2 g/dL, and troponin I 0.02 ng/mL, unchanged on repeat.
The chest radiograph showed pulmonary edema without a focal
infiltrate. An echocardiogram showed an ejection fraction of
58 percent, diastolic dysfunction, and moderate mitral
regurgitation. The fingers were warm during the exam, and
the Raynaud phenomenon was not active.

She received intravenous furosemide, with a net loss of 3.4
liters over three days. Oxygen was weaned from 2 liters by
nasal cannula to room air. Potassium was replaced by mouth.
Creatinine returned to 1.4 mg/dL. Carvedilol and losartan
were held on the first day and then resumed. Daily weights
fell from 61.2 kg to 57.6 kg. She slept flat enough to rest,
and Claire Ellison practiced the new diuretic plan at the
bedside before discharge on March 6, 2026.

Her weight on the morning of discharge was 57.6 kg. Blood
