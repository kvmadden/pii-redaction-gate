Acute exacerbation of chronic peripheral neuropathic pain and lumbar radiculopathy.

Secondary Diagnoses:
1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed in 2018.
2. Essential hypertension, diagnosed in 2015.
3. Lumbar spinal stenosis with L4-L5 degenerative spondylolisthesis.
4. History of idiopathic peripheral facial nerve palsy (Bell palsy), resolved.

History of Present Illness:
The patient is a 67-year-old female admitted for severe, refractory burning pain and paresthesias in the lower extremities radiating along the L5 dermatome bilaterally. The symptoms began insidiously several years ago but worsened markedly over the past month. She experienced intermittant sharp pains that disrupted sleep and walking. Lumbar magnetic resonance imaging performed at an Outside Hospital demonstrated moderate central canal stenosis and bilateral neural foraminal narrowing at the L4-L5 level. On arrival, she was in distress due to neuropathic shooting pains rated 9/10 on the numeric rating scale.

Hospital Course:
During the hospital stay, the care team coordinated acute pain stabilization and medication optimization. Physical therapy and occupational therapy evaluated the patient for safe ambulation and transfers. She was noted to be ambualting with a rolling walker under supervision. Given the neuropathic character of her discomfort, she was initiated on pregabalin (Lyrica) 75 mg PO BID, which was titrated upwards with significant symptomatic relief. In addition, an anti-inflammatory oral corticosteroid taper was administered for acute radicular nerve root irritation. Baseline neurological examination demonstrated normal mental status, intact cranial nerves without residual deficits from her remote Bell palsy, 5/5 motor strength in the bilateral upper extremities, and 4/5 strength in bilateral ankle dorsiflexion.
