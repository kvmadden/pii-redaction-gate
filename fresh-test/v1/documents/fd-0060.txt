Care coordination note

This letter asks Pain Clinic to accept a referral for a 71-year-old woman
with Parkinson disease diagnosed in 2016 and with lumbar radiculopathy
that has not improved after conservative care. The referral is to Pain
Clinic as a service of this health system, for consideration of a lumbar
epidural steroid injection. Records from Outside Hospital include the
operative note from a right knee arthroscopy and the images from a lumbar
MRI. Please request those outside records before the procedure visit
so the films can be compared with the local study.

Diagnoses relevant to this request are Parkinson disease, lumbar spinal
stenosis with radiculopathy, osteoarthritis of the right knee, and a
recent inflammatory flare of the knee. She had a right knee arthroscopy
several years ago and physical therapy afterward. Walking tolerance is
now limited to about one block. She describes burning pain down the right
leg to the dorsum of the foot, worse with standing. There is no bowel or
bladder incontinence, no saddle anesthesia, and no progressive weakness
that would suggest cauda equina syndrome. Straight leg raise increases
the leg pain. Strength is 5/5 at the hips and 4/5 at the right ankle
dorsiflexion. Resting tremor and mild bradikinesia are present and are
followed by neurology.

Current medications are carbidopa-levodopa 25/100 mg PO TID, rasagiline
1 mg PO daily, Lyrica 75 mg PO BID for neuropathic pain, acetaminophen
650 mg PO q6h PRN pain, and a short prednisone taper of 40/30/20/10 mg
PO daily, each dose level for three days, for the knee flare. Generic
pregabalin may be substituted for Lyrica if the strength is the same.
Please do not stop carbidopa-levodopa before the procedure without
speaking with neurology. Aspirin 81 mg PO daily is held only if the
injectionist requests it. She does not take an anticoagulant. The
covering pharmacist reconciled this list with the pill bottles recently
and found one extra bottle of acetaminophen.

Laboratory results drawn at the clinic: white blood cell count 7.2 K/uL,
hemoglobin 12.8 g/dL, platelets 214 K/uL, creatinine 0.8 mg/dL, eGFR 81
mL/min/1.73 m2, CRP 6.1 mg/L, and hemoglobin A1c 6.2%. Vitals at that
visit were blood pressure 136/78 mmHg, heart rate 64 beats per minute,
respiratory rate 16 breaths per minute, oxygen saturation 97% on room
air, temperature 36.6 C, and weight 68 kg. The knee was warm but not red.
There is no fever and no suspicion of septic arthritis.

The procedure requested is a lumbar epidural steroid injection under
flouroscopy, with a possible right L4-L5 transforaminal approach if the
MRI supports it. Diagnoses to link to the procedure are lumbar
radiculopathy and lumbar spinal stenosis. If Pain Clinic prefers a medial
branch block first, that alternative is acceptable to the referring
clinician. The patient has already completed a course of physical therapy
and a home exercise program. She uses a single-point cane. Her daughter
drives her to visits and can stay for teaching.

Falls have occured twice this season, both without head injury and
without fracture. Neurology believes the falls relate to shuffling gait
rather than to orthostasis. Blood pressure does not drop when she stands.
She should bring a current list of evening doses, because
carbidopa-levodopa timing is easy to confuse on a procedure morning when
she may be told to arrive without eating. If the service needs a caregiver
present for consent teaching, her daughter is willing. No interpreter is
required.

Please send the patient instructions that mention holding further
prednisone if the taper has already finished, continuing Lyrica 75 mg PO
BID the morning of the procedure unless told otherwise, and arranging a
ride home. She should call the clinic for new weakness, loss of bladder
control, headache with fever, or symptoms of very high glucose while on
prednisone. We can send a seperate summary of the neurology notes if Pain
Clinic needs them. Thank you for reviewing this referral. A reply with
the first available procedure day is enough to move scheduling forward.
The referring office will complete any form the service requires.

Care coordination
On behalf of the referring clinician
