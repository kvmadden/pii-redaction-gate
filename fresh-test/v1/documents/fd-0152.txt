The face weakness with a normal stroke workup fits Bell palsy, and the steroid course should be finished rather than stopped in the middle. Take prednisone 40/30/20/10 mg PO daily, two days at each step, then stop. Keep metformin 1000 mg PO BID with food. A morning glucose of 246 mg/dL is high and expected while prednisone is on board, and it should ease as the dose falls. For a 68-year-old with diabetes, I am placing a referral to Diabetes Center so the steroid-related highs can be watched. Your blood pressure at the last visit was 138/82 mmHg and the pulse was 92 bpm. Creatinine was 0.9 mg/dL and A1c was 7.8%, so metformin is still appropriate. Check a fingerstick before breakfast and again later in the day. Send a portal note if glucose stays above 300 mg/dL, if you have vomiting, or if the weak eye hurts or the vision blurs. Use lubricating eye drops, 1 drop to the weak eye QID while awake, and a bland ophthalmic ointment at bedtime. Protect the cornea, because the lid is not closing fully. Do not patch the eye, because a patch can rub the cornea. A short course of prednisone is the usual drug treatment for Bell palsy, together with that eye care.
