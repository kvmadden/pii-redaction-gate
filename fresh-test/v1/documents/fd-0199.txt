DISCHARGE SUMMARY

Principal diagnosis: aspiration pneumonia, improved, with a concurrent exacerbation of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.
Additional diagnoses: Parkinson disease, oropharyngeal dysphagia, and allergic rhinitis.
Disposition: home with his son after swallow teaching. A referral was placed to speech therapy and to pulmonology as outpatient services.

History of present illness. The patient is a 71-year-old man who came in with two days of fever, productive cough, and a choking spell while drinking water. Parkinson disease was diagnosed in 2017 and has caused a soft voice, a shuffling gait, and slowing of his swallow. A videofluoroscopic swallow study in 2022 showed delayed initiation and silent aspiration of thin liquids. He had been eating in bed. His son, who is 44 and lives nearby, noticed milder confusion than the patient's usual baseline and brought him in.

Past history. COPD has been present since 2014, with two exacerbations treated with steroids in recent years, though not with intubation. Allergic rhinitis is longstanding and is the reason he keeps Allegra at home. There is no heart failure, no prior stroke, and no gastrointestinal bleeding. He stopped tobacco years ago. Home medicines were carbidopa-levodopa 25/100 mg PO TID, tiotropium 18 mcg inhaled once daily, fluticasone-salmeterol 250/50 one inhalation BID, Allegra 180 mg PO daily, and aspirin 81 mg PO daily. He has no known drug allergy. The brand Allegra and the generic fexofenadine were both listed on old bottles, and only one was continued.

Examination and data. Temperature 38.4 C, blood pressure 128/72 mmHg, heart rate 104 beats per minute, respiratory rate 24 breaths per minute, and oxygen saturation 88 percent on room air, improving to 95 percent on 3 liters by nasal cannula. Crackles were heard at the right base. The examination fit Parkinson disease, with resting tremor, rigidity, and a stooped posture, and there was no new hemiparesis. White blood cell count 16.2 thousand per microliter, hemoglobin 13.4 g/dL, platelets 210 thousand per microliter, sodium 133 mmol/L, potassium 3.8 mmol/L, creatinine 1.0 mg/dL, glucose 142 mg/dL, and lactate 1.4 mmol/L. Procalcitonin was 0.8 ng/mL. Chest radiograph showed a right lower lobe infiltrate without a large effusion. Blood cultures were sent before antibiotics.

Hospital course. He was transfered from the emergency department to the ward on supplemental oxygen. Ceftriaxone 1 g IV q24h and azithromycin 500 mg IV daily were begun for pneumonia. Azithromycin was stopped when the legionella antigen came back negative, and ceftriaxone was changed to amoxicillin-clavulanate 875 mg PO BID to complete seven days. Prednisone was given as 40/30/20/10 mg PO each morning for the COPD flare, two days at each step, with food. Glucose stayed under 180 mg/dL without insulin. A bedside swallow evaluation was followed by a repeat videofluoroscopic swallow study, wich confirmed ongoing risk with thin liquids and safer handling of nectar-thick liquids and soft solids. Speech therapy practiced chin tuck and smaller bites. Oxygen was weaned to room air, and resting saturation stayed stabel at 94 percent. He walked with a walker and standby assist. Carbidopa-levodopa was kept on the home schedule. The covering pharmacist stopped the duplicate fexofenadine so that Allegra 180 mg PO daily was the only antihistamine. We reccomend that the son set out the levodopa doses and watch the evening meal.

Discharge medications.
Amoxicillin-clavulanate 875 mg PO BID for the remaining days of a seven-day course.
Prednisone 40/30/20/10 mg PO each morning, two days at each step, then stop.
Carbidopa-levodopa 25/100 mg PO TID.
Tiotropium 18 mcg inhaled once daily.
Fluticasone-salmeterol 250/50 mcg one inhalation BID.
Allegra 180 mg PO daily. Do not also take fexofenadine.
Aspirin 81 mg PO daily.
Albuterol 2 puffs inhaled every 4 hours as needed for wheeze.
Acetaminophen 650 mg PO every 6 hours as needed for fever.

Follow-up and precautions. He should return for worse dyspnea, new fever, chest pain, or confusion that comes back. Liquids at home should stay nectar-thick until speech therapy says otherwise. Meals are best taken upright, with no lying back for half an hour. His son will stay overnight for the first several nights. Pulmonology follow-up is requested to review whether the inhaled combination should continue, and neurology follow-up is requested for the Parkinson disease medicines. The instructions were read back by the patient and his son.

Signed by the discharging hospitalist for the inpatient team.
