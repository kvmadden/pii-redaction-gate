OUTPATIENT CONSULTATION NOTE
REASON FOR CONSULTATION: Evaluation of chronic bilateral lower extremity pain and suspected radiculopathy versus peripheral neuropathy.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:
The patient is a 64-year-old female who presents for comprehensive outpatient evaluation of worsening bilateral lower extremity burning discomfort, numbness, and paresthesias. Her symptoms initially began distally in both feet in 2018 and have gradually progressed proximally to mid-calf level over the intervening years. She describes the sensation as a persistent "fire-like" ache accompanied by intermittent sharp, electric shocks that intensify at night, significantly disrupting her sleep architecture. She was previously treated with gabapentin, which was discontinued due to excessive daytime sedation. In 2021, she was initiated on Lyrica 75 mg PO BID, which provided partial relief; however, over the past year her pain has escalated to an average severity of 7/10 on the visual analog scale.
Records transferred from Outside Hospital document an emergency department encounter in 2023 for acute right lower extremity swelling, at which time a Wells score was calculated at 0 points (low probability), and bilateral venous duplex ultrasound ruled out deep vein thrombosis. An electromyogram and nerve conduction study performed at that facility demonstrated findings consistent with distal symmetric sensorimotor polyneuropathy. Additionally, the patient reports a history of recurrent right-sided facial weakness that occurred in 2019, which was diagnosed as idiopathic Bell palsy and resolved almost completely following an oral steroid taper.
She denies bowel or bladder incontinence, saddle anesthesia, focal lower extremity weakness, fevers, chills, or unintentional weight loss. Her daughter accompanies the patient to the visit and confirms that balance has deteriorated mildly over the past six months, prompting the patient to utilize a single-point cane for prolonged ambulation.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN:
The patient is a 64-year-old female with longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus presenting with symptomatic progressive diabetic peripheral neuropathy, asesssed as inadequately controlled on current regimen.
1. Neuropathic Pain:
- Titrate Lyrica from 75 mg PO BID to 150 mg PO BID over the next two weeks. Advise patient regarding potential side effects including peripheral edema and somnolence.
- Referral placed to Pain Clinic for multimodal pain assessment, physical therapy evaluation, and consideration of adjunctive topical compounded therapy.

2. Diabetes Mellitus:

3. Preventative & Follow-up:
