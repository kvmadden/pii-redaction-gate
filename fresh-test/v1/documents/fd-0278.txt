Location: general internal medicine clinic
Author: attending physician, general internal medicine

Chief complaint: Follow-up of gout, heart failure, diabetes, and Parkinson disease.

History of present illness: The patient is a 71-year-old woman seen with her 46-year-old daughter, who fills the pillbox. Breathing is better than last week, when she slept on two pillows and had evening ankle swelling after prednisone for acute gout of the right first metatarsophalangeal joint. The taper was prednisone 40/30/20/10 mg by mouth over four days. The joint is now mildly sore. Fasting glucose often ran 180 to 220 mg/dL on the steroid, and weight rose about 1.5 kg, consistent with fluid. She denies chest pain, syncope, and fever. She describes occassional lightheadedness on standing for a few seconds, without palpitations or a fall. Parkinson disease, diagnosed in 2019, is steady, with right-hand rest tremor, soft speech, and slow gait, and without hallucinations. Morning cough with white sputum is baseline, and she uses an albuterol inhaler about twice a week. We recieved the echocardiogram report from Outside Hospital and reviewed it today. She is referred to Cardiology for the heart failure and to the Diabetes Center for insulin teaching. Pain Clinic was offered as a service for lumbar stenosis if walking limits her, and she prefers to wait. Appetite is fair. She denies melena and dysuria. One evening dose of diuretic was missed earlier in the week.

Past medical history: Parkinson disease diagnosed in 2019. Type 2 diabetes mellitus since 2014 with distal sensory neuropathy. Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction. Chronic kidney disease stage 3. Gout. Moderate COPD. Lumbar spinal stenosis. Osteoarthritis of the knees. Hypothyroidism. Former smoker, quit in 2008. Colonoscopy in 2018 with a small tubular adenoma. Laparoscopic cholecystectomy in 2011. Right cataract extraction in 2020. No known coronary disease.

Allergies: rash with sulfa antibiotics. Penicillin tolerated. Iodine contrast tolerated. No food allergy.

Medications: carbidopa-levodopa 25/100 mg PO TID, one hour before meals. pregabalin (Lyrica) 75 mg PO BID. fexofenadine (Allegra) 180 mg PO daily. furosemide 40 mg PO BID. empagliflozin 10 mg PO daily. metformin 1000 mg PO BID with meals. insulin glargine 16 units SQ qHS. lisinopril 10 mg PO daily. atorvastatin 40 mg PO qHS. levothyroxine 75 mcg PO each morning. tiotropium 2.5 mcg inhaled daily. albuterol HFA 2 puffs inhaled q6h PRN wheeze. allopurinol 100 mg PO daily. acetaminophen 650 mg PO q6h PRN pain. aspirin 81 mg PO daily. cholecalciferol 2000 units PO daily. polyethylene glycol 17 g PO daily. The prednisone 40/30/20/10 mg course is finished. Please keep the the evening diuretic seperate from the bedtime dose of carbidopa-levodopa.

Social history: Lives with her daughter in a single-level home. Retired clerical worker. No alcohol and no illicit drugs. Uses a cane indoors. Daughter helps with bathing and transportation.

Family history: Mother had a late-life stroke. Father had type 2 diabetes. No family history of Parkinson disease. One brother has gout.

Right first metatarsophalangeal joint mildly warm, no large effusion. Feet intact, with monofilament reduced at three of ten sites on each foot. Resting right-hand tremor, mild cogwheel rigidity, and reduced arm swing.

Assessment and plan: Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, near baseline volume. Continue furosemide 40 mg PO BID, with an extra furosemide 20 mg PO once if weight rises more than 1 kg for two mornings. Continue empagliflozin 10 mg PO daily. Parkinson disease. Continue carbidopa-levodopa 25/100 mg PO TID before meals. Do not increase the dose today, given the orthostatic drop. Physical therapy referral for gait. Avoid metoclopramide and antipsychotics.

Gout flare resolved. Prednisone 40/30/20/10 mg is complete. Continue allopurinol 100 mg PO daily and acetaminophen 650 mg PO q6h PRN. Call for a hot joint, fever, or inability to bear weight.

Chronic kidney disease stage 3, creatinine 1.32 mg/dL, stable. Continue lisinopril 10 mg PO daily. Pregabalin (Lyrica) stays at 75 mg PO BID for the level of renal function. Recheck chemistry in six weeks.

COPD. Continue tiotropium 2.5 mcg inhaled daily and albuterol HFA 2 puffs q6h PRN. No antibiotic.

Lumbar spinal stenosis with tolerable pain. No Pain Clinic referral today. That service remains available if walking declines.

Prevention. Schedule repeat colonoscopy, last done in 2018 after polypectomy of a tubular adenoma. Continue atorvastatin 40 mg PO qHS and aspirin 81 mg PO daily. Levothyroxine 75 mcg PO daily unchanged.

Follow up in the clinic in six weeks, sooner for gain in weight, worse dyspnea, syncope, or fasting glucose over 250 mg/dL. The covering pharmacist will go over the pillbox with her daughter, including the glargine change and the completed steroid course.
