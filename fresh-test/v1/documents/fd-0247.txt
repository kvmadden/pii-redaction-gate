HOSPITAL COURSE SUMMARY:
Ms. Winter M. Gebreselassie is a 94-year-old female with a history of moderate
chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and hypertension who presented on July
15, 2026 with a three-day history of escalating exertional dyspnea, chills, and
cough productive of thick yellowish sputum. Her 42-year-old son-in-law, Mateo
Castillo Gómez, noted that her mental clarity had declined and her oral intake
was minimal over the forty-eight hours prior to presentation.

Upon initial evaluation in the emergency triage unit, she was febrile with a
temprature of 38.7 C, tachycardic at 108 bpm, tachypneic at 26 breaths/min, and
hypoxemic with an oxygen saturation of 86% on ambient air, improving to 93% on
4 liters nasal cannula. Initial chest radiography and computed tomography of the
thorax revealed a dense consolidation within the right lower lobe accompanied by
a moderate free-flowing right pleural effusion. Initial arterial blood gas
demonstrated acute hypoxemic respiratory failure with pH 7.36, pCO2 46 mmHg, and
pO2 58 mmHg. Empiric broad-spectrum antimicrobial therapy was initiated with
intravenous ceftriaxone and azithromycin.

Given the significant pleural fluid collection, pulmonary consultation was
requested and performed promptly by Dr. Tariq
Mahmood. On July 17, 2026, Dr. Mahmood performed an ultrasound-guided bedside
thoracentesis, successfully removing 650 mL of amber, cloudy pleural fluid.
Pleural fluid analysis was consistent with an exudative, non-purulent effusion:
pleural protein 3.8 g/dL (serum 6.4 g/dL), pleural LDH 410 U/L (serum LDH 220
U/L), pH 7.34, glucose 78 mg/dL, and negative Gram stain. Bronchoscopy with
bronchoalveolar lavage was considered but deferred given rapid clinical
improvement following thoracentesis and antibiotic administration. Sputum and
pleural fluid cultures ultimately grew Streptococcus pneumoniae susceptible to
ceftriaxone and amoxicillin-clavulanate.

During the middle portion of the hospitalization, the patient received
scheduled nebulized albuterol-ipratropium and a brief five-day course of oral
prednisone 40 mg daily for her chronic obstructive pulmonary disease
