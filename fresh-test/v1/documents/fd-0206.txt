DIVISION OF RHEUMATOLOGY AND CLINICAL IMMUNOLOGY

REASON FOR CONSULTATION:
Evaluation and inpatient management of acute polyarticular rheumatoid arthritis flare, corticosteroid taper planning, and optimization of neuropathic pain.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:
The patient is a 56-year-old female with seropositive rheumatoid arthritis diagnosed in 2017, type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed in 2019, essential hypertension, and an episode of right-sided Bell palsy in 2021 that resolved completely. She was admitted to the inpatient medicine service three days ago following a severe multi-joint flare involving symmetrical swelling, warmth, and morning stiffness in bilateral wrists, metacarpophalangeal joints, proximal interphalangeal joints, and the right knee.

The flare began four weeks ago after a lapse in her baseline subcutaneous biologic regimen. Prior to admission, she was maintained on certolizumab pegol 200 mg subcutaneously every 2/3 weeks alongside oral hydroxychloroquine. Historical records and plain radiographs forwarded from Outside Hospital confirm symmetrical marginal erosions of the metacarpal heads and carpal joint space narrowing first identified in 2023.

On hospital day 1, she underwent diagnostic and therapeutic right knee arthrocentesis, yielding 38 mL of cloudy yellow synovial fluid. Synovial fluid analysis revealed a total nucleated cell count of 18,400 /uL with 78% polymorphonuclear leukocytes, negative Gram stain, and no birefringent crystals under polarizing microscopy, confirming inflammatory synovitis without septic arthritis or crystalline disease. Bacterial cultures show no growth.

Over the past 48 hours, the patient has reported burning dysesthesias over bilateral feet in a stocking pattern. Her 32-year-old daughter at the bedside confirms progressive nocturnal burning over six months and steroid-induced glucose elevations. The primary inpatient team requested consultation for corticosteroid tapering, neuropathic pain management, and specialty referrals to Diabetes Center and Pain Clinic.

3. Type 2 diabetes mellitus: Steroid-associated hyperglycemia.
4. History of Bell palsy: Resolved without residual deficit.
5. Low DVT probability: Wells score 0 points and duplex ultrasound negative.

PLAN AND RECOMMENDATIONS:
1. Rheumatoid Arthritis Management and Corticosteroid Taper:
- Initiate oral prednisone 40/30/20/10 mg daily taper: administer prednisone 40 mg PO daily for 7 days, 30 mg PO daily for 7 days, 20 mg PO daily for 7 days, then 10 mg PO daily for 7 days, followed by 5 mg PO daily maintenance until outpatient rheumatology re-evaluation.
- Continue hydroxychloroquine sulfate 200 mg PO BID.
- Maintain hold on certolizumab pegol until completion of prednisone taper; resume certolizumab pegol 200 mg subcutaneously every 2/3 weeks under outpatient rheumatology supervision.
- Pantoprazole sodium 40 mg PO daily for gastric protection.
2. Neuropathic Pain Optimization:
- Increase Lyrica from 75 mg PO BID to 150 mg PO BID.
- Continue Allegra 180 mg PO daily.
- Outpatient referral submitted to Pain Clinic for multidisciplinary pain rehabilitation.
3. Metabolic Management:
- Outpatient referral to Diabetes Center for glycemic titration and education during steroid taper.
4. Venous Thromboembolism Prophylaxis:
- Enoxaparin sodium 40 mg subcutaneous once daily while inpatient; discontinue at discharge given ambulation and Wells score of 0.
5. Follow-Up:
- Outpatient follow-up with Rheumatology in 3 weeks, Diabetes Center in 2 weeks, and Pain Clinic within 4 weeks. If pressure injury occurs, referral to Wound Care will be arranged. Records from Outside Hospital have been archived.
