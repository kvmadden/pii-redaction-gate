DEPARTMENT: AMBULATORY CARE PHARMACY AND CLINICAL TRIAGE
COMMUNICATION TYPE: POST-DISCHARGE RECONCILIATION AND CARE COORDINATION

TRIAGE NURSE: Good morning, clinical triage line. This is the triage nurse on duty. How can I assist pharmacy today?

COVERING PHARMACIST: Hello, good morning. This is the covering pharmacist from the outpatient clinical team. I am calling to review the transition of care orders and discharge documentation recieved from Outside Hospital for a 54-year-old patient who was recently evaluated for acute unilateral facial paralysis and diagnosed with Bell palsy. The clinical notes also noted pre-existing focal neuropathic pain diagnosed in 2021. I noticed a few discrepancies on the discharge medication reconciliation and wanted to clarify the dosing schedule and prior authorization status with the clinical team before sending the maintenance prescriptions through for dispensing.

TRIAGE NURSE: Thank you for following up on this. I have teh scanned chart and the consultation packet from Outside Hospital open in front of me right now. The patient was initially evaluated in the emergency department and then admitted to the clinical observation unit before being discharged home in stable condition. What specific questions did you have regarding the medication regimen?

COVERING PHARMACIST: First, regarding the systemic steroid therapy for the acute Bell palsy. The inpatient team initiated an oral corticosteroid taper, but the discharge summary and the electronic discharge order transmitted to the pharmacy contained conflicting directions. The written discharge summary recommended a standard prednisone 60/40/20/10 mg taper over twelve days, but the electronic order was entered as prednisone 40 mg PO daily for five days without tapering instructions. Given the severity of the facial weakness at presentation, the standard clinical protocol recommends the higher initial dose burst followed by a gradual reduction: prednisone 60 mg PO daily for five days, then 40 mg PO daily for two days, 20 mg PO daily for two days, and 10 mg PO daily for two days. Has the attending physician confirmed which taper schedule we should follow?

TRIAGE NURSE: Yes, let me look at the clinical addendum entered this morning. The attending physician reviewed the outside records and confirmed that the prednisone 60/40/20/10 mg taper is indeed the intended course. In addition, the current orders include valacyclovir 1000 mg PO TID for seven days for antiviral coverage, plus preservative-free artificial tears 1 drop in the right eye q2h PRN while awake, and lubricating ophthalmic ointment 0.25 inch to the right eye at bedtime. Because the patient cannot achieve full right eyelid closure, corneal protection is critical right now.

COVERING PHARMACIST: Excellent, that provides clear direction on the acute regimen. Next, I wanted to address the neuropathic pain therapy. According to the medical history dating back to 2019, the patient has chronic peripheral neuropathy. During the observation stay, the inpatient team started Lyrica 75 mg PO BID. However, the pre-admission medication profile still includes gabapentin 300 mg PO TID. This represents dual gabapentinoid therapy. Combining Lyrica and gabapentin creates an unnecessary risk of additive central nervous system depression, marked somnolence, dizziness, and peripheral edema. My clinical recommendation is to discontinue the outpatient gabapentin completely and continue the Lyrica monotherapy, titrating upward as tolerated.

TRIAGE NURSE: That makes total sense. I will submit an order modification to discontinue gabapentin 300 mg PO TID and keep Lyrica 75 mg PO BID as the single agent for neuropathic pain. The primary care provider also placed an electronic referral to Pain Clinic for comprehensive multidisciplinary evaluation once the steroid taper is completed. Has the patient's health plan cleared the Lyrica prescription, or will an appeal be required?

COVERING PHARMACIST: The electronic pharmacy adjudication system initially rejected the prescription claim, requesting prior authorization due to formulary step-therapy requirements. However, because the historical records confirm documented trials and therapeutic failure of both gabapentin and duloxetine since 2022, we have strong justification. I am preparing the prior authorization appeal documentation right now. Could you please share the most recent vital signs and laboratory findings from the clinical records so I can attach them to the justification package?

TRIAGE NURSE: Absolutely. At the discharge evaluation, vital signs were documented as follows: blood pressure 126/82 mmHg, heart rate 70 bpm, respiratory rate 16 breaths per minute, body temperature 98.4 F, and oxygen saturation 99% on room air. The patient was alert, oriented, and experiencing no systemic symtoms. Laboratory results from the hospital admission were: fasting blood glucose 106 mg/dL, hemoglobin A1c 5.9%, serum sodium 140 mEq/L, serum potassium 4.2 mEq/L, blood urea nitrogen 15 mg/dL, serum creatinine 0.84 mg/dL, estimated glomerular filtration rate 96 mL/min/1.73 m2, aspartate aminotransferase 24 U/L, alanine aminotransferase 28 U/L, and complete blood count showing a white blood cell count of 7.8 x10^3/uL, hemoglobin 14.1 g/dL, and platelet count 245 x10^3/uL. Renal and hepatic parameters are completely normal, so no dose adjustment is necessary for Lyrica.

COVERING PHARMACIST: Those laboratory values are very helpful and support the clinical need. Were there any diagnostic imaging studies or specialty procedures completed during the hospitalization?

TRIAGE NURSE: Yes. Diagnostic magnetic resonance imaging of the brain and internal auditory canal with intravenous gadolinium contrast was performed at Outside Hospital. The radiologist reported prominent enhancement of the right facial nerve within the geniculate ganglion and tympanic segments, entirely consistent with acute Bell palsy, without evidence of intracranial mass, acute ischemia, or vascular compression. High-resolution computed tomography of the temporal bone showed clear mastoid air cells and intact bony architecture. The neurology consultant documented a House-Brackmann score of grade IV facial paresis and advised follow-up electromyography in three weeks if clinical recovery is delayed.

COVERING PHARMACIST: Perfect. I will assemble the prior authorization request with the clinical summary, imaging reports, and renal panel, and submit it directly to the health plan. In the meantime, I will dispense the prednisone 60/40/20/10 mg taper, valacyclovir 1000 mg PO TID, and the ocular lubricants for immediate courier delivery so there is no delay in treating the Bell palsy.

TRIAGE NURSE: Thank you so much for your thorough review and for streamlining this transition. I will alert the primary care team and place a note in the electronic chart. Have a wonderful rest of your shift.

COVERING PHARMACIST: You too. Thank you, goodbye.
