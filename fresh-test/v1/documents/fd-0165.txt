MEDICATION RECONCILIATION AND PRIOR AUTHORIZATION

The covering pharmacist completed this review by telephone after a
syncope admission. Records from Outside Hospital supplied the
administration list and the discharge diagnoses. The patient is a
69-year-old man. His son brought every bottle from the house and stayed
for the teaching.

Reason for review
He was admitted after a brief loss of consciousness at home with prompt
recovery. The Glasgow Coma Scale was 15 on arrival and stayed there.
Telemetry showed atrial fibrillation with ventricular rates mostly under
100 and no prolonged pause. An echocardiogram showed a normal ejection
fraction, no large effusion, and left atrial enlargement. Orthostatic
vital signs were positive, and the working judgment was volume depletion
plus rate-related lightheadedness rather than a primary neurologic
event. He has Graves disease, diagnosed in 2018, still treated with an
antithyroid drug, along with hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus,
stage 3 chronic kidney disease, and gout. A shin ulcer has been open for
months. Allopurinol has been on the list since 2020.

During the stay a gout flare began in the right great toe. Nonsteroidal
anti-inflammatory drugs were avoided because of kidney disease and
because he takes an anticoagulant. Colchicine was also held to avoid
stacking interacting drugs. A short steroid taper was chosen instead.
Cardiology will follow the syncope and the atrial fibrillation as a
service. Wound Care will follow the ulcer.

Prednisone will be dispensed as 10 mg tablets: four tablets, then
three, then two, then one, each for four days. That matches the
40/30/20/10 mg taper and totals forty tablets. He should not stretch
the course because the toe feels better early.

Apixaban stays at 5 mg rather than 2.5 mg. Dose-reduction criteria that
combine advanced age with kidney function are not met, and body weight
is above 60 kg. Do not add aspirin for the arrhythmia. If a dose of
apixaban is missed, he should not double the next one.

Brand Allegra was requested on an old bottle. The son agreed to generic
fexofenadine at the same dose and schedule, which avoids a needless
prior authorization.

The ulcer is cleaned with saline and covered with a nonadherent foam
under compression arranged by Wound Care. No topical antibiotic was
added. Debridement of loose fibrin was done during the admission and is
the procedure of record. An ankle-brachial index before compression was
adequate, so compression stays appropriate.

Counseling and monitoring
Hold empagliflozin and metformin on days he cannot eat or drink, then
restart when intake is normal, to limit ketoacidosis and lactic
acidosis. Hold lisinopril if vomiting persists. He should rise slowly
and sit before walking because the standing pressure drops. On
methimazole, fever, sore throat, or mouth ulcers need a same-day call
because of agranulocytosis. Propranolol should not be stopped abruptly.
Keep a steady apixaban schedule and avoid naproxen, ibuprofen, and
aspirin. Allopurinol continues through the flare at the current dose
and is not increased now. Acetaminophen is the analgesic for the toe
and for ulcer pain. Prednisone will raise glucose, so fingerstick
checks increase until the taper ends. The clinic should hear about
repeated readings above 250 mg/dL. Fexofenadine is preferred to
diphenhydramine, which would add fall risk. The son repeated the taper
counts correctly. A thirty-day supply of the chronic medicines was
prepared, with prednisone limited to forty tablets. No colchicine and
no nonsteroidal drug were dispensed.
